# U.S. New-Grad Resume Tailoring Google Docs

Create a targeted, truthful, one-page resume for a U.S. new graduate that is publication-ready in Google Docs. The final document is a new standalone Google Doc in the user's My Drive root, unless they name another location.

## Required inputs and intake

Require a current or master resume and the target job description. Accept relevant supplemental accomplishments when the user offers them. Do not request a formatting reference; use the standard in [resume standards](references/resume-standards.md).

Before drafting, determine the role's dominant theme and analyze the job description for responsibilities, must-have and preferred capabilities, keywords, technical requirements, customer or user context, and scope. Compare each important requirement to the supplied evidence as strong, partial, or missing.

Never fabricate evidence, inflate ownership, or change an official title without confirmation. When a missing requirement or material resume detail is ambiguous, ask a focused question before finalizing. If the user cannot support a required capability, omit the claim rather than forcing the keyword. If the dominant role theme differs from AI product building, briefly state the resulting story shift and tailor to that theme.

## Job-fit and professional resume evaluation

Before proposing revisions, run an independent evaluation of the resume against the job description. Identify the role theme; the capabilities, keywords, and outcomes the employer values; the candidate's strongest relevant evidence; transferable but indirect experience that can be reframed truthfully; and the highest-priority evidence gaps. For product and product-adjacent roles, assess whether the resume demonstrates identifying business or user needs, making product or technical decisions, collaborating across functions, validating work, and producing measurable outcomes.

Evaluate the draft from both an ATS and a hiring-reader perspective: relevance to the role, credible scope and ownership, clarity of the value delivered, outcome and metric quality, evidence of judgment, readable storytelling, and ease of scanning. Share the high-level positioning and only the material gaps or focused evidence questions before drafting when they would change the result.

Offer the user an optional opportunity to paste feedback from an external resume reviewer or PM-resume tool. Do not require it and do not wait for it if the user declines or does not provide it. Treat external feedback as advice, not authority: compare it to the job description and source evidence, adopt only helpful supported recommendations, and never fabricate claims to follow it.

## Content approach

Use the strongest supported narrative for the target role. When it is supported by both the role and the evidence, position the candidate as someone who identifies business and user needs and uses AI or technology to address them. The default sequence is need, workflow or problem understanding, product or technical action, validation, and impact; adapt it when the role calls for another central story.

Write concrete accomplishment bullets. Each should state a supported action and outcome, generally fit within three lines, and use an accurate ownership verb. Favor measurable business or user impact, direct relevance, customer discovery, technical implementation, product judgment, evaluation, and cross-functional execution over generic skill lists.

Treat page utilization as a content-quality problem, not merely a formatting check. When there is room, strengthen the resume with supported evidence that makes the candidate more compelling for this specific role. Revisit the job-description gap analysis and choose the strongest missing or underrepresented story: what the candidate did, why it mattered, and what changed. A bullet may add useful context about the project, decision, implementation, or impact when that context improves the reader's understanding of fit; do not add detail simply to fill space.

For students and recent graduates, default to Education, Work Experience, AI and Technical Project Experience, Leadership and Awards, and Skills. Include GPA under five years of full-time experience. Do not add a summary unless the user has more than seven years of full-time experience.

## Review, then publish

Before publishing, create a one-page staging document and render it to a clear page image or PDF. Show that preview in the conversation (and provide the staging file if useful), then invite the user to request content or layout changes. Treat the displayed preview as a review checkpoint: do not upload or create the Google Doc until the user approves it, unless they explicitly ask to skip review or publish immediately.

When the user approves, create a new Google Doc named `[Name] - [Company] - [Role] Resume`. Preserve the user's selected folder if one is specified; otherwise use My Drive root. Use the approved local DOCX only as a staging artifact when necessary for reliable construction, then import or create the standalone Google Doc and make Google Docs the final layout authority.

Apply the formatting and QA requirements in [resume standards](references/resume-standards.md). After publishing, render or inspect the completed Google Doc and revise it until it matches the approved content, is exactly one page, readable, balanced, and compliant. If the cloud rendering requires a material layout or content change, show the revised preview and obtain approval before declaring it final. Do not append it to the user's master resume.

Upload, create, or modify cloud documents only with the user's authorization and verify the final document is visibly present. Report the Google Doc result and any evidence gaps that remain unresolved.
